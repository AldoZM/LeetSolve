- Maximum K to Sort a Permutation, Minecraft edition. LeetCode problem thirty six forty four, and a neat bit trick.
- Seven ore blocks, numbered zero to six, all mixed up. Under each one, three redstone lamps show its bits.
- We may swap two blocks only when their bitwise AND is exactly k.
- Find the largest k that still lets us sort the whole row. Here, the answer is one.
- The direct idea is to try every k, from the top down.
- For each k, test every pair of blocks to see which swaps are allowed, then check if the row can be sorted.
- Six values of k, twenty one pairs each. That's already a hundred twenty six checks, and our pickaxe is gone.
- With n blocks, the work grows like n cubed.
- Here's the trick. A block that is out of place has to move, so it must be part of some swap.
- And a swap only works when every bit of k is lit in both blocks.
- So k has to fit inside every misplaced block. The biggest k that does is the AND of all of them.
- So we walk the row once, with a panel of lamps that starts fully lit. That panel is our running AND.
- Zero is already home. Skip it.
- Three is out of place. AND it in, and the top lamp goes dark.
- Two is home. Five is not. Now only the last lamp survives.
- Four is home. One is misplaced, but its last bit is lit, so the panel stays at one.
- Six is home. Advancement made. The answer is k equals one.
- One pass over the row, and one AND per block. That's linear time.
- The panel is a single number, so the extra space is constant.
- One loop, one check, one AND. If nothing is misplaced, we simply return zero.
- Only the blocks out of place decide k. That's the big lesson of Maximum K.
