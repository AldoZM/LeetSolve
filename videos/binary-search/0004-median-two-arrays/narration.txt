- Median of Two Sorted Arrays. LeetCode number four, and one of the most famous hard problems.
- You get two sorted arrays, and you need the median of all their numbers combined.
- Here there are eleven numbers in total, so the median is the sixth smallest. That's eleven.
- The easy way merges both arrays, then picks the middle. But that takes linear time.
- The problem asks for log time. So we can't even look at every number.
- Here's the key idea. The median splits all the numbers into a left half and a right half.
- If we cut A at some point i, the cut in B is forced, because the left side must hold exactly six numbers.
- So we only binary search the cut in the shorter array.
- The cut is valid when everything on the left is at most everything on the right. Since both arrays are sorted, we only check across the cut.
- Let's search. The cut in A can go anywhere from zero to five.
- Try the middle, i equals two. That forces j to four. The left side gets two numbers from A, and four from B.
- Check across the cut. Nineteen on the left is bigger than eight on the right. So the cut in A is too far left.
- Move right. Now i is four, and j is two.
- Nine is at most eighteen, and eleven is at most fifteen. Both checks pass, so the cut is valid.
- The total is odd, so the median is the largest value on the left. The max of nine and eleven is eleven.
- We binary search only the shorter array, so the time is log of the smaller length.
- And we only keep a few pointers, so the space is constant.
- No merging. Just one cut, checked across both arrays.
- Find the right cut, and the median falls out.
