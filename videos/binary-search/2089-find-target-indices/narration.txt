subtitles:
- Find Target Indices After Sorting Array, Minecraft edition. LeetCode problem twenty eighty nine, and a sneaky shortcut.
- Six ore blocks in a row, and a target. Two.
- If we sorted the row, where would every two end up? Return those positions.
- Sorted, the row reads one, two, two, two, three, five. So the answer is one, two and three.
- The direct idea is to sort the whole row, then scan it for the twos.
- Sorting puts every block in order, even the three and the five, which we never needed in order.
- That costs n log n, when the answer only depends on two counts.
- Here's the trick. In the sorted row, every block smaller than the target comes first.
- So the twos start right after them. With one smaller block, the first two lands at index one.
- And with three twos, they fill three slots in a row from there.
- Let's walk the row once, with two counters. Less, and equal.
- Five is bigger. Skip it. Two is the target. Equal is one.
- One is smaller. Less is one. Two again. Equal is two.
- Three is bigger. Then the last two. Equal is three.
- The twos start at index less, which is one, and take equal slots, which is three.
- Advancement made. The answer is one, two, three, without sorting a thing.
- One pass with two counters. That's linear time.
- And besides the answer, we only keep two numbers. Constant extra space.
- Count the smaller ones, count the matches, and read off the range.
- Sometimes you don't need the order, only the counts. That's the big lesson of this problem.
