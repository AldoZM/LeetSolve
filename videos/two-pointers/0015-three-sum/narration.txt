- Three Sum, Minecraft edition. LeetCode problem fifteen, an interview favorite.
- We get a row of numbers, some of them negative. Find every group of three that adds up to zero.
- Each triple counts once, even if a number repeats. Here there are two.
- The direct idea is to try every triple of blocks.
- Three nested loops. Pick one, pick a second, pick a third, and check the sum.
- With six blocks, that's twenty triples. And we still have to remove the repeated ones.
- It grows cubically. A thousand blocks means over a hundred and sixty million triples.
- Here's the trick. Sort the row first. Then fix one block, and find the other two with two pointers.
- Sum too small? Move the left pointer right. Too big? Move the right pointer left.
- Sorted, the row is minus four, minus one, minus one, zero, one, two.
- Fix minus four. Every pair is too small, so the left pointer runs all the way. Nothing.
- Fix minus one. Minus one, plus minus one, plus two makes zero. Found one!
- Both pointers step in. Zero plus one plus minus one is zero too. Found another!
- The next minus one is the same value as before, so we skip it.
- Fix zero. One plus two is too big, and the pointers meet. Done.
- Advancement made. Two triples. Minus one, minus one, two. And minus one, zero, one.
- Sorting costs n log n. Each fixed block walks the pointers once, so the total is n squared.
- From n cubed down to n squared, and sorting makes repeats easy to skip.
- Sort, fix one, and squeeze with two pointers.
- Sorting turns a search into a squeeze. That's the lesson of Three Sum.
