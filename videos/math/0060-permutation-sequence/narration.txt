subtitles:
- Permutation Sequence, Minecraft edition. LeetCode problem number sixty, and a brilliant math puzzle.
- We have four ore blocks from one to four. In total, they form twenty four unique permutations in order.
- Our target sign asks for permutation number nine. Which sequence of blocks is it?
- The direct idea is backtracking. Recursively generate every single permutation from scratch.
- Mine each combination branch by branch until reaching number nine.
- For four blocks, it is twenty four. But with nine blocks, that is three hundred sixty two thousand permutations!
- Our pickaxe shatters trying to dig through so many dead ends. Factorial growth is brutal.
- Here is the trick. Notice the pattern: with four blocks, fixing the first block leaves six choices for each.
- Six start with one, six with two, and so on. We can jump directly to the right group with division!
- We switch to zero-based indexing, so target nine becomes index eight. Our chest will store the final sequence.
- Divide eight by three factorial, which is six. Quotient is one, remainder is two.
- Quotient one picks index one from our remaining blocks: that is the iron block, two!
- Now with blocks one, three, and four, divide remainder two by two factorial. Quotient one picks three!
- Remainder is zero. Divide by one factorial: zero over one is zero, picking block one.
- Only block four remains, so it drops into the final slot without any guessing.
- Advancement made! The ninth permutation is two, three, one, four.
- Instead of factorial time, we compute each digit in just one math step.
- The total time is n squared, taking mere microseconds, and memory is strictly linear.
- Precompute the factorials, pick each element with division and modulo, and remove it from the list.
- Don't generate what you can calculate. That is the power of the factorial number system.
