- Regular Expression Matching, Minecraft edition. LeetCode problem ten, a hard one.
- We get a text, a a b, and a pattern, c star a star b. Does the pattern match the whole text?
- A dot matches any one letter. A star means zero or more copies of the letter before it.
- The direct idea tries every choice. Each star matches zero letters, or one more.
- Every choice splits into two branches, and each branch keeps splitting.
- And the same small questions get asked again and again, in different branches.
- In the worst case, that's exponential time. Our pickaxe won't survive it.
- Here's the trick. Every question has the same shape. Does the text from i match the pattern from j?
- Answer each pair once, and store it in a table. That's dynamic programming.
- Our table is a grid. Rows are spots in the text, columns are spots in the pattern.
- The bottom right corner is true. An empty text matches an empty pattern.
- We fill it backwards. A letter matches if both letters are equal and the rest matches, one step diagonal.
- A star has two options. Skip it, two columns right. Or use one letter, one row down.
- With just b left, b matches b, and both stars can be skipped. That row lights up.
- With a b, the a star uses its a. With a a b, it uses one more.
- The top left corner lights up. Advancement made. The pattern matches the whole text.
- Each cell takes constant time, so the time is m times n.
- The table holds m times n answers. Exponential becomes polynomial.
- Two loops, two cases, and one table.
- When the same question repeats, answer it once and store it. That's dynamic programming.
